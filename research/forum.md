So every statement about how the test was built comes from the **official pages** (`comp_info/pages_content.md`). The forum adds nothing to them. Verbatim official text:

> "The dataset consists of self-contained paragraphs generated by 18 language models across diverse topics. Every passage is an authentic LLM completion. Only light formatting cleanup was applied (for example, removing a `**bold**` wrapper around a whole passage)."

> "test.csv — ... ~4000 passages in the hidden rerun. Test passages are written for prompts that do not appear in train.csv."

> "Pinpointing the exact source model ... is much harder when prompts are unseen and passage lengths vary randomly between 40 and 250 words. You can't rely on simple character-count shortcuts or topic keywords."

> "The number of test passages per model is **not** equal, and the mix of models differs between the public and private leaderboard. Balanced accuracy gives every model the same weight."

> Rules 18.f: "The 'Public Leaderboard' is a ranked display of Participants' Submission scores against a representative sample of the test data."

> Rules 18.a: "The Competition Data will contain private and public test sets. Which data belongs to which set will not be made available to Participants."

> Prompt topics: "prompts covering philosophy, physics, mathematics, biology, computer science, and creative prose."

What follows from the official text (my inferences, **not** host-confirmed):
- Class counts are explicitly **unequal** in test. Train is balanced (2699 ≈ 150×18; one passage missing). So test cannot simply be "every prompt × all 18 models". Some models are subsampled or dropped per prompt, or some prompts have more passages. **Do not assume a strict 1-per-model-per-prompt structure in test.** That rules out a hard Hungarian or assignment decoding over prompt clusters. A soft prior or a partial assignment could still help, because the per-prompt structure probably still holds approximately.
- At about 18 passages per prompt, ~4000 passages means roughly 220 test prompts.
- The public and private model mixes differ, so prior or centering tricks fitted to the public LB class mix may not carry over to private. "Adaptive centering", which estimates the label prior from the test batch itself, is safer than hard-coding a prior tuned on the LB.
- The length distribution is described as "vary randomly between 40 and 250 words". The reconstructed-prompts post also says each generation got its own random target length, presumably drawn independently of the model, so length carries no label signal.
- Hosts give no statement on whether test prompts are grouped or on system prompts and temperature.

## External data and paid API generation: rules text, no ruling

The rules allow external data if it is public and free to all, OR meets the "Reasonableness" standard. Verbatim:

> 6.a "You may use data other than the Competition Data ("External Data") to develop and test your Submissions. However, you will ensure the External Data is either publicly available and equally accessible to use by all Participants of the Competition for purposes of the competition at no cost to the other Participants, or satisfies the Reasonableness criteria as outlined in Section 2.6.b below."

> 6.b "The use of external data and models is acceptable unless specifically prohibited by the Host. Because of the potential costs or restrictions ... their use must be "reasonably accessible to all" and of "minimal cost". ... The Reasonableness Standard will be determined and applied by the Host in light of things like cost thresholds and accessibility."

> 6.b.2 "By way of example only, a small subscription charge to use additional elements of a large language model such as Gemini Advanced are acceptable if meeting the Reasonableness Standard ... Purchasing a license to use a proprietary dataset that exceeds the cost of a prize in the competition would not be considered reasonable."

> Code requirements: "Freely & publicly available external data is allowed, including pre-trained models."

> 5.3: "In the event that input data or pretrained models with an incompatible license are used to generate your winning solution, you do not need to grant an open source license ... for that data and/or model(s)." (relevant to non-commercial datasets)

> Private sharing: "Privately sharing code or data outside of Teams is not permitted. It's okay to share code if made available to all Participants on the forums."

**Community view, not official:** Ravi Ramakrishnan in 749545: "That is the open secret here - augment the data using your own money and keep augmenting". jazivxt notes the Kaggle Benchmarks offer of about $100/month in API credits. Yurnero and wsc-MISX say external data use is effectively unenforceable (they cite RSNA and PetFinder). Yan Zhou: "Banning external data is really hard to enforce anyway." The top-voted thread (749447, 70 votes) is the "pay to win" complaint. Nobody, host or otherwise, has stated that generated data must be published publicly during the competition. In practice, the safest compliant path is public datasets: Oleg's corpus, PersonalLLM and other public HF datasets. Self-generated API data is a grey zone; if it is used, plan to publish it.

## Technical content in the threads

### 1. CV and LB correlation (Aleksey Osipenko, 749593): the only confirmed CV/LB number
> "Every prompt in train is answered exactly once by each of the 18 models (2,699 passages / 150 prompts). That makes a random K-fold misleading – but in the opposite direction from usual. With a random split, the passage you validate on has its 17 "siblings" (same prompt, other models) in train. Topic words were seen only with other labels, so they push the prediction away from the true model."
> "Same TF-IDF + stylometry model, balanced accuracy: StratifiedKFold (random): 0.217 / StratifiedGroupKFold by prompt_id: 0.369 / Public LB of the same model: 0.355"
> "The lab is much easier than the exact model: lab-level balanced accuracy 0.587 vs 0.369. About half of all errors stay inside the same lab – the three Gemini Flash models confuse each other most."
> "Both GLM models are most often mistaken for claude-opus-5."

Notebook: https://www.kaggle.com/code/alekseyosipenko/name-that-llm-honest-cv-by-prompt-stylometry (already mirrored locally as `research/kernels*/0-355-name-that-llm-honest-cv-by-prompt-styl`).
Takeaway: **GroupKFold by prompt_id tracks the LB within about 0.014.** Random-split CV underestimates by about 0.15 because topic words in a held-out passage were seen in train only with other labels.

### 2. Reconstructed train prompts (pjmathematician, 749455)
- Sonnet 5 was used to reverse-engineer the instruction behind each of the 150 prompt_ids. Kaggle dataset "Name That LLM – Reconstructed Train Prompts", mirrored at `research/datasets_meta/pjm`.
- Prompt style: pop-science "explain X vividly". 85 explain-a-concept, 45 describe/narrate, 10 argue/opinion, 8 story/fiction, 1 compare. Domains: physics 32, biology 28, philosophy 21, creative prose 16, math 15, history 12, CS 10, psychology 6, economics 5, other 5. The topics are specific named items (Jevons paradox, Stern–Gerlach, Hilbert's hotel, ...). Style modifiers ("vivid", "poetic", "haunting", "use an everyday analogy") are common.
- "Answers are always one self-contained paragraph with no headings, and often share an opening phrase across models."
- "Length varies inside each prompt (about 40 to about 250 words for nearly every prompt). That fits the host's note that each generation got its own random target length."
- Use: this is a template for generating more in-distribution data (same prompt style plus a random target length of 40–250 words), and for prompt-matched external data selection.

### 3. 18-model external corpus (Oleg Zholobov, 749629, 12 votes)
- Wide format: id · prompt · 18 model-name columns. 135,171 unique responses and 70,980 wide rows in total. **24,015 responses (13,642 rows) in the 40–250-word range.** 1,902 prompt groups have all 18 models.
- Sources: "Mostly from public Hugging Face datasets, GitHub evaluation logs, etc." (Artificial Analysis, Novigens, APIFlow, JetBrains, RISE). License metadata is kept per source, and "Source licenses vary".
- No exact or normalized overlap with the 2,699 train answers, and no near-matches at 90% similarity.
- "Start with external_short_wide.parquet." "A separate Luna quarantine has weaker labels". Treat the gpt-5.6-luna labels as noisy.
- "For validation, group by prompt and keep duplicate responses together. Shared prompts may have different system/history settings."
- Oleg has not tried SFT on it yet ("not yet"). Local mirror: metadata only (README, manifest, source_catalog) in `research/datasets_meta/oleg` and `research/datasets/response-corpus`; the parquet files have not been downloaded locally yet.
- Caveat: the style and length distribution differ from the competition's single-paragraph pop-science answers, so a domain-shift risk remains. In 749447, Ayush Khaire warns that self-generated data may not match the hidden test's generation protocol.

## Warnings about overfitting the public LB
- No explicit forum post covers this. The official warning is that the model mix differs between public and private and that class counts are unequal. Combined with the ~0.014 noise seen between group CV and LB, gaps of 0.01–0.03 on the public LB are probably noise. Choose by prompt-grouped CV.

## Practical implications for our plan (goal: 1st place, open to changing the architecture)
1. Validate only with GroupKFold on prompt_id. It is shown to track the LB.
2. The biggest lever the community sees is **more labeled data**, and nobody has posted results from it yet. A first-mover advantage is available with Oleg's public corpus (13.6k in-range rows; luna labels are noisy) and possibly self-generated API data. The latter is a grey area: no host ruling yet, so if it is used, publish it to stay compliant.
3. Avoid hard-coding a public-LB class prior. Test-time prior estimation (EM, adaptive centering) is acceptable because the private mix differs.
4. Hierarchical lab-then-model modelling could help. Lab-level accuracy is much higher than model-level, and the Gemini Flash trio plus GLM → claude-opus-5 are the main confusions.
5. Use per-prompt clustering of test only as a soft constraint. Do not assume each model appears exactly once per test prompt.
6. Re-check the forum before relying on API-generated data. A host ruling on 749613 / 749664 could appear at any time.
